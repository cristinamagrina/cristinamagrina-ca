Online Parent Counseling

Providing online parent counseling to families in Toronto and across Ontario, Quebec, and Nova Scotia.

This is a space for you: your experience, your struggles, and your growth as a parent, at any stage of your child's life.

What Brings Parents to Therapy

  • Parenting stress, overwhelm, and burnout
  • Postpartum adjustment and the transition to parenthood
  • Anxiety about your child's well-being or development
  • Difficulty setting boundaries or managing conflict with your child
  • Co-parenting, separation, divorce, or blended family dynamics
  • A desire to be a better parent to your child or children
  • Parenting a child with ADHD, anxiety, OCD, or other challenges
  • Navigating adolescence and the shift toward independence
  • Rebuilding or redefining your relationship with an adult child
  • Balancing parenting with career, relationships, or your own identity
  • Guilt, self-doubt, or feeling like you're “getting it wrong”

What the process looks like

01

How Sessions Work

Sessions are collaborative and grounded in evidence-based approaches, adapted to your family's specific needs and your child's age and stage. Together, we might work on understanding your child's behaviour in a new way, building confidence in your parenting approach, strengthening communication and connection with your child, managing the stress or overwhelm that comes with parenting, navigating co-parenting or family transitions, reflecting on your own upbringing and how it shapes your parenting, and adjusting your approach as your child grows and changes.

This isn't about following a single “right” way to parent. It's about finding an approach that reflects your values and works for your family.

If you're curious about holistic parenting, looking at your child's emotional, social and physical well-being as a whole, my article on holistic strategies for parents and caregivers is a good place to start.

02

What to Expect From the First Session

The first session is a chance for us to get to know each other. We'll talk through what's bringing you to therapy, some context about your family and your child, and what you're hoping to get out of the process.

There's no pressure to have a clear diagnosis of the problem going in — we'll figure out the right direction together.

03

Where We Go From There

From there, we'll build an approach that fits your family, and check in regularly to make sure therapy continues to meet your needs as your child (and you) continue to grow.

What We Might Work Toward

Every parent's goals are different. Depending on what brings you here, we might work toward:

  • Feeling less overwhelmed and more equipped to manage parenting stress
  • Understanding your child's behaviour with more clarity and less reactivity
  • Feeling more confident and less second-guessing in your parenting decisions
  • Communicating more effectively with your child, co-parent, or family
  • Navigating transitions — new baby, adolescence, separation, an adult child leaving home — with more resilience
  • Feeling more like yourself again, not just “a parent”

Therapy isn't about becoming a perfect parent. It's about understanding yourself and your child more fully, and building an approach that feels sustainable and true to who you are.

Frequently asked questions

Is online therapy suited for parents?

Yes, online therapy is a well-supported and often especially practical option for parents, who are frequently juggling childcare, work, and little spare time.

Sessions from home mean one less thing to coordinate around.

Do you work with parents of children of any age?

Yes, I support parents at every stage, from the early newborn years through adolescence and into adulthood. Parenting challenges look different at each stage, but they're all valid reasons to seek support.

Is this the same as the parent consultations offered alongside my child's therapy?

No, parent consultations that accompany a child's therapy are focused specifically on supporting that treatment.

This is separate: therapy for you, as a parent, whether or not your child is also in therapy.

Can we work on my relationship with my adult child?

Yes, parenting doesn't end when your child turns 18. Many parents come to therapy to navigate changing dynamics with adult children, whether that's setting healthier boundaries, repairing a strained relationship, or adjusting to a more independent role.
